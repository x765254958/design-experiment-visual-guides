# Experiment intake

## Minimum brief

Collect or infer the following before freezing the design:

1. Research question and intended claim.
2. Material, drug, cell, strain, tissue, or animal identity.
3. Available sample batches and which controls belong to each batch/system.
4. Available equipment, outsourced tests, training limitations, and deadlines.
5. Candidate assays and which outcome is primary.
6. Dose basis: total particle mass, active-drug equivalent, molarity, area dose, extract ratio, or released amount.
7. Treatment duration and sampling times.
8. Existing laboratory SOPs, kit manuals, ethics approvals, and biosafety restrictions.
9. Desired deliverables: plan, Word manual, plate map, visual guide, slides, or all.

## High-impact questions

Ask only questions that can change the design. Examples:

- Is the active ingredient identity/form confirmed?
- Are loaded and blank materials from the same synthesis system and batch logic?
- Is actual loading known, or are concentrations only total-particle mass?
- Is the purpose screening, optimization, mechanism, or formal publication data?
- Are technical replicates and independent repeats both possible?
- Which procedures are already approved and trained in the laboratory?

## Parameter ledger

Maintain a table with columns:

| Parameter | Value | Status | Source | Consequence if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell line | L929 | confirmed | user/lab | changes culture conditions |
| Particle dose | 15–100 µg/mL | provisional | pilot design | changes safety range |

Statuses: `confirmed`, `literature`, `provisional`, `unknown`.

## Stop asking when

Proceed with explicit assumptions when missing information changes only presentation, not experimental validity. Stop and ask when the answer changes safety, groups, dose basis, primary endpoint, sample pairing, or feasibility.

# Safety and governance gate

## Always confirm

- laboratory authorization and training;
- organism/cell/sample identity;
- institutional SOP and containment level;
- chemical hazards and required PPE;
- waste and decontamination route;
- animal or human ethics approval where applicable.

## Live microorganisms

Provide detailed operational steps only for work the user is authorized and trained to perform in an approved laboratory. Preserve the laboratory's strain-specific SOP, containment, sterilization, and disposal instructions. Do not guess a pathogen's handling category or propose procedures that increase virulence, host range, resistance, environmental persistence, or transmission.

## Cell culture and primary material

Require confirmed aseptic handling, cell identity, contamination checks, and approved material sterilization. Do not assume filtration, autoclaving, or UV treatment is compatible with a nanoparticle or drug.

## Animals and humans

Do not convert a conceptual study into an executable animal or human protocol without ethics approval, trained personnel, humane endpoints, analgesia/anesthesia plans, and local veterinary/clinical SOPs. Keep preliminary planning at the design level until these are confirmed.

## Hazardous chemistry

Use SDS and institutional chemical-hygiene rules for solvent, dust, corrosive, oxidizer, and nanomaterial handling. State uncertainty when ventilation, respiratory protection, or disposal depends on local assessment.

## Wording rules

Prefer:

- “provisional starting condition”
- “confirm with the laboratory SOP”
- “requires pilot validation”
- “not established from the available information”

Avoid:

- “according to regulations” when no regulation was supplied;
- “standard concentration” without a named source;
- “safe” based on a single assay;
- “successful synthesis” based on one characterization method.
